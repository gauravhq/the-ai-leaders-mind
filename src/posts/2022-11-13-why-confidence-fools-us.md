---
title: "Why Confidence Fools Us"
date: 2022-11-13
pageDescription: "We read confidence as competence. A machine has the first without the second."
---
There is a wiring problem worth knowing about. People treat confidence as evidence of competence. When someone states a thing plainly and without hedging, we lower our guard and tend to believe them; it is a fast, usually useful shortcut.

A machine breaks the shortcut. It produces confident language regardless of whether it is right, because certainty is not a feeling it has to earn. It cannot be nervous. So the usual signal we lean on, that steadiness means knowledge, points the wrong way.

Once you see this, you can correct for it. When an answer arrives sounding sure, treat the sureness as noise, not information. Ask what sits underneath it. A confident wrong answer and a confident right answer look identical on the screen.

The leaders who work best with these tools are the ones who stopped being reassured by tone. They learned to want the reasoning, the source, and the limits, and to feel most cautious exactly when the machine sounds most certain.
