---
title: "Availability in Hiring"
date: 2023-04-02
pageDescription: "The vivid candidate crowds out the better one. So can the vivid data point."
---
Hiring is where availability bias does quiet damage. The candidate who sticks in your mind is not always the strongest one; it is the one with the memorable story, the sharp line in the interview, the detail that stuck. Vividness, not fit, wins the mental competition for attention.

A model does not fix this on its own, and can feed it. It surfaces what it can measure, and what it measures becomes what feels important. The traits that do not fit neatly into the data, judgment, temperament, how someone treats people under stress, drop out of the picture because nothing on the screen is pointing at them.

The correction is to decide what actually matters for the role before you look at anyone, and hold to it. Write down the qualities the job truly needs, then judge each person against that list rather than against the last vivid impression.

Vividness is a bad proxy for value. The quiet candidate who fits may beat the memorable one who does not, and only a deliberate standard, set in advance, will let you see it.
