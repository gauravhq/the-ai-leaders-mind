---
title: "Who Owns the Error"
date: 2023-10-08
pageDescription: "When a tool gets it wrong, a person still has to answer for it. Decide who in advance."
---
When an automated decision goes wrong, there is a moment where everyone looks around and no one is quite responsible. The tool made the call, or informed it, and the humans nearby all feel one step removed. That gap is dangerous, and it is best closed before the error, not after.

Someone has to own the outcome. Not the model, which cannot be accountable, and not a vague collective, which means no one. A specific person who will answer for the decision, in their own name, as if the tool had only advised.

Naming that person in advance changes behavior before anything goes wrong. An owner reviews more carefully, questions the tool more honestly, and does not hide behind it, because they know the buck stops with them. Diffuse responsibility produces diffuse attention, and diffuse attention is how bad automated decisions slip through.

So for any decision that matters, decide who owns it before you make it. The tool can carry the analysis. It cannot carry the responsibility, and pretending it can is how organizations end up with failures that belong to everyone and therefore to no one.
