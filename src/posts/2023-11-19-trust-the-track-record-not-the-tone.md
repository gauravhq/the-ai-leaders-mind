---
title: "Trust the Track Record, Not the Tone"
date: 2023-11-19
pageDescription: "How sure a tool sounds tells you nothing. How often it has been right tells you everything."
---
There are two things you could base your trust in a tool on. How confident it sounds, and how often it has actually been right. Only one of them is information, and it is the boring one.

Tone tells you nothing. A model sounds equally certain when it is correct and when it is inventing, because confidence is its default register, not a signal it earns per answer. Leaning on tone means being reassured by the tool most precisely when you should be most careful, on the fluent, forceful, wrong answer.

Track record is the real basis. Where has this tool been reliable, and where has it burned you? That history, honestly kept, is what should set your trust, and it often points the opposite way from tone. The tool can be quietly excellent at a task it describes tentatively, and confidently terrible at one it states with force.

So build your trust on the record, not the delivery. Notice how sure it sounds, then set that aside and ask how often it has been right about this kind of thing. The tone is theater. The track record is the fact.
