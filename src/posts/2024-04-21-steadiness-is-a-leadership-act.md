---
title: "Steadiness Is a Leadership Act"
date: 2024-04-21
pageDescription: "In a period of fast change, a calm leader is doing real work, not just feeling calm."
---
Calm is not just a personal state during upheaval. It is a leadership act, because a team takes its emotional temperature from the person in charge, and in a period of fast, frightening change, your steadiness is doing real work whether or not it feels like it.

When the ground is shifting and people are anxious, a leader who is visibly rattled multiplies the anxiety. A leader who is genuinely steady, not faking calm but actually grounded, gives people something solid to stand near. That steadiness is not passivity. It is one of the most useful things you can offer.

This is why the inner work matters practically, not just personally. The practices that keep you steady, the focus on what you control, the longer view, the rehearsed preparation, are not self-indulgence. They are what let you supply the calm the team needs to function.

So treat your own steadiness as part of the job, not a nice-to-have. Build it deliberately, because a rattled leader spreads the rattling and a grounded one spreads the ground. In a moment of real uncertainty, the ability to stay steady, and to let people see it, is leadership in one of its plainest and most valuable forms.
