---
title: "The Accuracy-Score Fallacy"
date: 2024-06-16
pageDescription: "Seventy percent accurate answers a technical question, not a moral one."
---
A model is seventy percent accurate, so you proceed. It sounds reasonable, even rigorous. It is also one of the most common ways ethics quietly leaves an automated decision, because the number answered a technical question and got treated as if it answered a moral one.

Seventy percent accurate means thirty percent wrong, and the moral weight is hiding entirely in that thirty percent. Who are they? What happens to them? A denied loan, a missed diagnosis, a rejected application, a wrongful flag. The accuracy figure says nothing about how bad the errors are or who absorbs them, and those are the questions that actually matter.

So an accuracy score is a starting point for the ethics, never the end of it. Before you accept a number as good enough, ask what the errors cost and who pays. Sometimes a high accuracy rate is fine. Sometimes it hides harm concentrated on the exact people who can least push back.

The fallacy is letting a performance metric stand in for a moral judgment. The number tells you how often the tool is right. It does not tell you whether being wrong that often, to those people, is acceptable. That part is still yours.
