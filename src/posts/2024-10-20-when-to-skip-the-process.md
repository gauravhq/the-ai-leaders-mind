---
title: "When to Skip the Process"
date: 2024-10-20
pageDescription: "Most decisions are small. Save the full method for the ones that carry weight."
---
A decision framework is only useful if you know when not to use it. Run the full method on every trivial choice and you grind to a halt, and people rightly abandon a process that treats the color of a slide like a bet-the-company call. Most decisions are small, and small decisions should stay fast.

The layers of a careful decision, the model's input, your read, the gap, the human check, the owned call, are for the decisions that carry weight. The ones that affect people, commit real resources, or cannot easily be undone. On those, the few minutes the method costs are cheap. On the hundred small calls that fill a day, the same method is just friction, and friction where it is not needed teaches people to skip it everywhere.

So sort before you decide how much process to apply. Is this reversible and low-stakes? Move fast, let the tool help, do not overthink it. Is it consequential or hard to undo? Slow down and run the layers.

The skill is not applying the framework to everything. It is knowing which decisions deserve it, spending your care where it counts, and letting the rest move quickly. A method used everywhere becomes bureaucracy. A method used on the decisions that matter becomes judgment.
