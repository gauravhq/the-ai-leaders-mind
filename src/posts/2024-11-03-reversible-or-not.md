---
title: "Reversible or Not"
date: 2024-11-03
pageDescription: "The single most useful question before you decide how carefully to decide."
---
Before you decide anything, one question sorts most of the difficulty. Can this be undone? Reversible and irreversible decisions deserve completely different treatment, and confusing the two is behind a large share of both wasted time and real damage.

Reversible decisions should move fast. If you can change course cheaply once you learn more, then deliberating at length is a waste, and leaning on the tool to decide quickly is exactly right. Try it, watch what happens, adjust. The cost of being wrong is small, so speed wins.

Irreversible decisions are the opposite. When a choice cannot be walked back, or only at great cost, the value of care goes way up, and the fast confident answer becomes most dangerous precisely because it feels the same as all the reversible ones. The stakes changed and the interface did not.

So ask the question first, every time. Reversible? Move fast, let the machine help, do not agonize. Irreversible? Slow down, run the human checks, own the call carefully. Most decisions that cause real harm are irreversible ones handled at the speed of reversible ones, because no one paused to ask which kind they were facing before the tool swept them along.
