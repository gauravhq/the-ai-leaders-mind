---
title: "The Disagreement Protocol"
date: 2024-11-24
pageDescription: "When you and the model disagree, have a habit for what happens next."
---
The moment you and a tool disagree is the most valuable and most fumbled moment in working with it. Valuable because the disagreement points at something one of you is missing. Fumbled because most people resolve it by reflex, usually deferring to the machine, and the reflex throws away the value. What helps is having a habit for what happens next.

The habit is simple. When your read and the model's diverge, do not immediately pick a winner. First, name why you think what you think. Then look hard for what the model might be seeing that you are not. Then look for what you might know that it cannot. Only after that do you decide, deliberately, rather than by default.

The point of a protocol is to override the reflex. Left alone, disagreement resolves toward whoever feels more authoritative, and the confident tool usually wins. A habit forces the pause where the actual thinking happens.

So build the protocol into how you work. Disagreement is not a problem to end quickly. It is a signal to slow down and examine, because it is precisely where the machine and your judgment are seeing different things, and one of those differences is often the thing that would have saved you from a mistake.
