---
title: "The Update Habit"
date: 2025-03-02
pageDescription: "Treat your beliefs about the tools, and your work, as things to revise on schedule."
---
Beliefs go stale, and beside fast-moving tools they go stale fast. What was true about a system last year, its strengths, its limits, the right way to use it, may not be true now, and a leader running on outdated beliefs makes outdated decisions with full confidence. The fix is to treat updating your beliefs as a habit rather than something that happens by accident.

Most people update only when forced, when a belief fails so visibly it cannot be ignored. By then the outdated belief has already cost them. The update habit is more deliberate, a regular practice of asking what you believe about your tools and your work, and whether it is still true.

This runs against the comfort of settled opinions. It is pleasant to have decided how things are and stop reconsidering. But settled opinions in a shifting field are just errors waiting to be discovered.

So build the habit of revisiting. Periodically ask what you assume about the tools you rely on, and test whether it still holds. Notice where your mental model and reality have drifted apart. Treat your beliefs as provisional, subject to revision as evidence comes in. The willingness to update, on purpose and on schedule, is what keeps your judgment matched to a world that will not hold still, and it is a habit, not a talent, which means anyone can build it.
