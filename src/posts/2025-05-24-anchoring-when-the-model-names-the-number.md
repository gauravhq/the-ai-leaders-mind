---
title: "Anchoring: When the Model Names the Number First"
date: 2025-05-24
pageDescription: "Whoever names the first number wins more than they should, even when it is a machine."
---
Whoever says the first number wins more than they should. That is anchoring, and it does not care whether the first number came from a person or a machine.

When a model estimates the deal at twelve million, or scores the candidate at seventy-one, that figure becomes the gravity the rest of the conversation orbits. Everyone argues in its neighborhood. The question quietly shifts from what is right to how far from the anchor we are willing to move.

The machine makes this worse in two ways. It produces precise numbers, and precision reads as authority. Seventy-one point four sounds like it was earned, even when it was guessed.

Protect the decision by setting your own anchor first. Form a rough estimate before you open the tool. Write it down. Then let the model speak. If the two are far apart, that gap is information worth chasing, not a signal to quietly abandon your number for the tidier one on the screen.
