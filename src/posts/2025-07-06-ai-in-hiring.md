---
title: "AI in Hiring"
date: 2025-07-06
pageDescription: "The model can rank the candidates. It cannot tell you who to hire."
---
Hiring is where the temptation to defer to a tool is strong and the cost of doing so is high. A model can screen resumes, score candidates, surface patterns. What it cannot do is decide who to hire, because hiring is a judgment about a whole person joining a team, and a whole person is exactly what the data leaves out.

The dangers are specific. The model learned from past hiring, so it can quietly reproduce the patterns of who was hired before, including the ones you meant to move past. It scores what it can measure and ignores what it cannot, the judgment, the temperament, how someone treats people under stress. And its precise scores carry an authority that shuts down the human conversation that hiring actually needs.

So use it as one input, held at arm's length. Let it help you handle volume and surface candidates. Keep the decision human, informed by the tool and owned by a person. Ask who the model might be filtering out unfairly. And remember that the number it produces is a starting point for judgment, never a substitute for the harder question of who this person actually is and whether they belong here.
