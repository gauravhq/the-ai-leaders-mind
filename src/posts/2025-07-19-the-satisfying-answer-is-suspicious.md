---
title: "The Satisfying Answer Is Suspicious"
date: 2025-07-19
pageDescription: "Comfort is not evidence. An answer that feels perfect deserves a second look."
---
Pay attention to how an answer feels, because the feeling carries information the content hides. Real analysis usually lands with a little friction. There is something you did not want to hear, a complication, a cost. That friction is often the texture of truth.

An answer that feels purely satisfying, that confirms exactly what you hoped with no rough edges, deserves suspicion for that reason alone. Not because it is certainly wrong, but because comfort and correctness are different things, and a tool that can tell you what you want to hear will happily do so.

This is especially true when you came to the model already wanting an outcome. Ask it a leading question and it hands back a pleasing answer, and the pleasure makes you stop checking.

So when a result feels too good, slow down rather than speed up. Ask what it conveniently leaves out. Look for the cost it did not mention. The answers that save you are often the ones that annoyed you first. The frictionless ones are where trouble likes to hide.
