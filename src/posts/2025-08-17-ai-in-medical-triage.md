---
title: "AI in Medical Triage"
date: 2025-08-17
pageDescription: "A tool that sorts patients can save time and cost lives if trusted blindly."
---
Medical triage is a setting where the stakes make the principles vivid. A model can help sort patients, flag urgency, surface patterns a tired clinician might miss. Used as support, it saves time and catches things. Trusted blindly, it can cost lives, because the cases where it is wrong are exactly the cases where blind trust is fatal.

Automation bias is the specific danger. A clinician who defers to the triage score can miss what their own training would have caught, because the screen spoke first and the human judgment went quiet. The tool is right often enough to be trusted, and the day it is wrong is the day someone needed the clinician to be looking, not deferring.

So the model informs and the clinician decides, with their judgment fully engaged rather than parked. The score is a second opinion, valuable and fallible, to be weighed against what the person in front of them is actually showing.

This is the pattern for every high-stakes use, drawn in sharp relief because lives are on the line. The tool extends human capability and must never replace human judgment. A clinician using the model as support is stronger than one without it. A clinician deferring to it is weaker than one who never had it, because they have traded their own attention for a screen, at exactly the moment attention mattered most.
