---
title: "AI in One-on-Ones"
date: 2025-08-31
pageDescription: "The one conversation you should never let a tool run for you."
---
Tools now offer to help with one-on-ones, to generate talking points, summarize prior meetings, suggest what to raise. Some of that is harmless preparation. But the one-on-one itself is close to the last thing you should let a tool run for you, because its entire value is that it is a human moment, and a human moment routed through a machine stops being one.

The point of a one-on-one is connection, attention, the sense that this person and their work matter enough for your real presence. A conversation you are conducting from a tool's script, half-attending while it prompts you, delivers the opposite message, that they warranted a generated agenda rather than your actual thought.

People can feel the difference immediately. A one-on-one where you are genuinely present, thinking about them, listening, lands completely differently from one you are processing.

So keep this one human. Prepare however you like, but when you are in the room, be in the room, not managing a tool. The one-on-one is where trust is built or eroded in small regular increments, and it is built by presence, which is exactly the thing a machine cannot supply and can easily get in the way of. This is a conversation to have yourself, fully, every time.
