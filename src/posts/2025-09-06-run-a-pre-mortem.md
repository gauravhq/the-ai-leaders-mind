---
title: "Run a Pre-Mortem"
date: 2025-09-06
pageDescription: "Imagine the decision already failed, then ask why, before you commit."
---
One simple exercise beats most analysis. Before you commit to a decision, imagine it is a year later and the choice failed badly. Then ask, in that imagined world, what went wrong.

It works because it flips the question your mind wants to avoid. Left alone, you look for reasons your plan will succeed, which is exactly the plan you already like. The pre-mortem forces the other search, for the failure modes you have been quietly stepping past.

This is a good place to use the machine well. Ask it to make the case for how the decision goes wrong, to list the ways this fails. A tool that can argue any side is useful precisely here, turned against the plan instead of flattering it.

The pre-mortem does not make you gloomy. It makes you prepared. You surface the risks while you can still do something about them, instead of discovering them in the wreckage. Ten minutes imagining the failure can save you the year it would take to live through it.
