---
title: "The Manager as Meaning-Maker"
date: 2025-09-06
pageDescription: "The model gives answers. You give the why that makes people care."
---
A machine can tell people what to do. It cannot tell them why it matters, at least not in a way they believe. Meaning is made between people, and supplying it is becoming one of the manager's most important jobs precisely as the answers get automated.

People do not give their best to a task because a system assigned it. They give it because they understand how it connects to something they care about, and because someone they trust helped them see the connection. That someone is you. The tool can generate the plan. You make the plan mean something.

This is not soft or optional. Work stripped of meaning is work done at the minimum, and no amount of algorithmic efficiency fixes a team that has quietly stopped caring. The efficiency just gets you faster mediocrity.

So as the machine takes over more of the what and the how, lean harder into the why. Help people see the purpose behind the tasks, the person behind the metric, the point behind the work; that is not a thing a model can do for you, and it is a large part of why you are still needed.
