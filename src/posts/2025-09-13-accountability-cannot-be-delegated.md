---
title: "Accountability Cannot Be Delegated"
date: 2025-09-13
pageDescription: "You can hand a machine the analysis. You cannot hand it the responsibility."
---
You can delegate almost anything to a capable tool. The analysis, the draft, the recommendation, the first pass at a hard problem. The one thing that does not transfer is accountability. When the decision affects real people, someone human still has to answer for it, and that someone is not the machine.

It matters because the tool makes delegation feel total. It did the work so thoroughly that it feels as if it also carries the weight, and a leader can drift into treating the model's involvement as a kind of shared responsibility. It is not. A model cannot be accountable, cannot be answerable, cannot stand in front of the person it got wrong.

So keep the responsibility explicitly with a person, even when the work is mostly the machine's. Someone owns the outcome, in their own name, as if the tool had only advised.

The moment accountability blurs is the moment care leaves the decision. A choice that belongs to no one gets no one's full attention. Keep it owned, and the ownership will pull the attention the decision deserves.
