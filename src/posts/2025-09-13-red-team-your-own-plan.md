---
title: "Red-Team Your Own Plan"
date: 2025-09-13
pageDescription: "Ask the model to attack the thing you love, before someone else does."
---
The strongest thing you can do with a capable model is aim it at your own idea. Not to polish the plan you already like, but to try to break it.

Most people use the tool the other way. They bring it a conclusion and ask for support, and it obliges, because it can argue almost anything. That feels good and teaches you nothing. You end up with a well-dressed version of what you walked in believing.

Red-teaming flips it. Ask the model for the strongest case against your plan. Ask what a smart critic would say, where the weak assumption hides, what has to be true for this to fail. Then sit with the answers instead of swatting them away.

This is uncomfortable, which is the sign it is working. The aim is to meet the plan's flaws in a quiet room now, rather than in front of a customer or a board later. Use the tool's endless fluency to argue against yourself; it is the one use where being able to argue anything is a gift.
