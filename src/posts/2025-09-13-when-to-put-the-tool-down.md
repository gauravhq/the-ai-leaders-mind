---
title: "When to Put the Tool Down"
date: 2025-09-13
pageDescription: "Some conversations need no screen between two people."
---
There are moments when the most capable thing you can do is close the laptop. Some conversations are made worse by any tool in the room, because the tool becomes a place to hide, and the person across from you can feel you hiding.

You know the moments. Someone is upset, or afraid, or telling you something hard. The instinct is to reach for structure, to look something up, to let a screen mediate the discomfort. That instinct protects you and abandons them. What they need is your undivided attention, and a tool splits it by design.

Putting the tool down is a signal as much as an act. It says this conversation is about you, not a task, and I am fully here for it. People register that immediately, and it does more for trust than anything you could have looked up.

The skill is knowing which moments these are, and having the discipline to disconnect for them. Most of the day, the tools help. In the moments that decide whether someone feels genuinely met, they get in the way. Learn to tell the difference, and then put it down.
