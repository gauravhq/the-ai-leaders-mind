---
title: "AI in Everyday Email"
date: 2025-09-21
pageDescription: "Let it draft the routine, but never let it be your voice where your voice matters."
---
Email is where most people first let a tool write in their voice, and it is worth being deliberate about where that helps and where it quietly costs you. For routine, logistical, low-stakes messages, a tool that drafts well is a genuine time-saver, and no one is worse off for it. For the messages that carry weight, letting the machine be your voice is a mistake.

The messages that matter, the apology, the hard feedback, the note of real thanks, the difficult conversation, derive their value from being genuinely yours. A recipient who senses that a meaningful message was generated feels the absence immediately, and it costs you trust, because now they wonder about all your messages.

So sort your email by weight. The routine can flow through the tool. The messages that carry real human content have to be yours, in your actual voice, meaning what they say.

The trap is that the tool makes it so easy to generate everything that the meaningful messages get generated too, out of habit and speed. Resist that at the emotional edges of your communication. A shorter message you actually wrote beats a polished one the machine produced, whenever the whole point was that it came from you. Use the tool for the logistics, and keep your voice where your voice is the thing that matters.
