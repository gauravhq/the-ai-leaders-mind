---
title: "When to Deliberately Slow Down"
date: 2025-10-17
pageDescription: "Reversible calls can go fast. Irreversible ones earn a pause."
---
Not every decision deserves the same care, and pretending otherwise just makes you slow. The useful question is whether a call can be undone.

Reversible decisions can move fast. If you can change course cheaply once you learn more, speed is a friend, and leaning on the tool to move quickly is exactly right. Try it, watch, adjust.

Irreversible decisions are different. When a choice cannot be walked back, or only at great cost, the value of a pause goes way up. These are the calls where a confident, fast answer is most dangerous, because the speed feels like the others and the stakes are not.

So sort your decisions by that one test before you decide how much to trust the machine's pace. Fast and reversible, let it run. Slow and irreversible, put in the brake yourself, because the tool will not.

Most mistakes that hurt come from treating an irreversible call like a reversible one, and moving at the speed of the screen instead of the stakes.
