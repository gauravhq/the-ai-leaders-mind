---
title: "The Vendor You Never Met"
date: 2025-11-15
pageDescription: "Someone made choices inside the tool you rely on. You should know what they were."
---
Every tool you use was built by people who made choices, and those choices are now quietly shaping your decisions. What the model optimizes for, what data it learned from, where its makers decided good enough was good enough. You inherited all of it, usually without being told.

That matters because the vendor's judgment is now part of your judgment. When you act on the tool's output, you are trusting not just the machine but the unseen people who shaped it, and their priorities were not necessarily yours.

You cannot audit everything, but you can stop treating the tool as if it fell from the sky. Ask what it was built to do well, and for whom. Ask where it is known to be weak. Read what its makers say about its limits, and notice what they do not say.

A tool is not neutral infrastructure; it is a set of someone else's decisions, wearing the appearance of objectivity. Knowing whose choices you are relying on is part of relying on them well.
