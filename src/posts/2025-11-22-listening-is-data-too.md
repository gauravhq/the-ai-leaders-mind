---
title: "Listening Is Data Too"
date: 2025-11-22
pageDescription: "What a person tells you in the room outranks the report about them."
---
We have started treating data as the real information and conversation as something softer, less rigorous, less trustworthy. That is backward often enough to be dangerous. What a person tells you directly, in their own words, is data, and frequently better data than the report.

The dashboard shows you what can be counted. A person in the room can tell you what the numbers mean, what is about to happen that has not shown up yet, what the metric is missing. They carry context no system has captured, because they live inside the situation the data only samples.

So do not let the report override the person. When the numbers say one thing and someone close to the work says another, that disagreement is not the person being wrong; it is a signal worth chasing, because they may be seeing what the data cannot.

Listening well is a form of measurement, and a leader who stops doing it in favor of dashboards is trading rich information for tidy information. Keep asking people directly, and keep taking what they say seriously. It is data, and it is often the data that matters most.
