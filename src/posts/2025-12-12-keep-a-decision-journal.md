---
title: "Keep a Decision Journal"
date: 2025-12-12
pageDescription: "Write what you decided and why, so you can learn what the tool did to your thinking."
---
A small practice pays off out of all proportion. On decisions that matter, write down what you decided, what you expected to happen, and how much the tool shaped the call. A few lines is enough.

The value shows up later. Memory rewrites itself to make you look wiser than you were, and it quietly hides the influence the machine had. A journal does not. It lets you go back and see where the model helped, where it led you astray, and where you deferred when you should have pushed.

Over time this builds the one thing calibration needs, an honest record. You stop guessing whether to trust the tool on a given kind of call and start knowing, because you kept score.

It also keeps you in the habit of forming a view. The act of writing your reasoning forces you to have reasoning, which is exactly the muscle a fast answer tempts you to skip. The journal is cheap. What it teaches you about your own judgment is not.
