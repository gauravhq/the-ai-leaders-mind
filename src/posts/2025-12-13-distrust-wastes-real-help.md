---
title: "Distrust Wastes Real Help"
date: 2025-12-13
pageDescription: "The opposite error is quieter but real: refusing help a tool has genuinely earned."
---
We spend a lot of worry on over-trust, and there is a matching error that gets less attention. Under-trust. Refusing to rely on a tool that has genuinely earned it, redoing by hand what it would do better, treating caution as a virtue when it has become a waste.

This is not the same as healthy skepticism. Skepticism checks and then uses. Under-trust checks and then discards, over and over, never letting the tool carry what it has proven it can carry. It feels responsible and it is just slow.

The cost is real. A leader who trusts nothing the machine produces spends their scarce attention re-deriving things that were already right, and has less left for the calls that actually needed a human. Blanket doubt is as lazy as blanket faith. Both refuse the work of calibration.

So watch for the tool you never trust as closely as the one you trust too much. If it has been reliable on a kind of task again and again, and you are still redoing it by hand, that is not diligence; it is help refused.
