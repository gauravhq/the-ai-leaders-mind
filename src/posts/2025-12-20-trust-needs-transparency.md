---
title: "Trust Needs Transparency"
date: 2025-12-20
pageDescription: "You cannot calibrate trust in a box you cannot see into."
---
Trust and understanding are linked. You can trust a person whose reasoning you cannot see because you know their character over time. A tool has no character to know, so trust in it depends much more on being able to see how it works, at least a little.

A model that shows nothing but an answer is hard to calibrate, because you have no way to tell a sound conclusion from a lucky one. When it is right, you do not know why, so you cannot predict when it will be wrong. You are trusting a box, and a box can only be trusted blindly or not at all.

So value transparency and ask for it. What did the recommendation rest on? What was it uncertain about? What data shaped it? The more you can see, the more finely you can trust, relying where the reasoning is sound and doubting where it is thin.

This is why a slightly less accurate tool that explains itself can be more useful than a more accurate one that does not. Understanding is what turns raw output into something you can trust with judgment, rather than faith.
