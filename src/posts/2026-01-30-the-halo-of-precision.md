---
title: "The Halo of Precision"
date: 2026-01-30
pageDescription: "A decimal point makes a guess look like it was earned."
---
Precision reads as authority. A model that says seventy-one point four sounds more credible than a person who says roughly seventy, even when the round number is the more honest one. The extra digits feel like extra work, extra rigor, extra truth. Often they are none of those things.

A machine can produce precise numbers out of shaky inputs without any of the discomfort a person would feel doing the same. It does not hedge. So a rough estimate comes out wearing the costume of a measurement, and the costume does a lot of persuading.

The move is to look past the digits to what produced them. How was this number reached? What is it sensitive to? Would it move a lot if one assumption changed? A precise figure built on a guess is still a guess, no matter how many decimals it carries.

Distrust precision that has not been earned. The tidiest number in the room is not automatically the truest, and treating it that way is how a guess gets mistaken for a fact.
