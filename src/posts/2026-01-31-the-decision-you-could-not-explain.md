---
title: "The Decision You Could Not Explain"
date: 2026-01-31
pageDescription: "If you cannot say why, you did not make the call. The machine did."
---
Think of the last decision you made with a tool that you could not fully explain afterward. Someone asked why, and you found yourself pointing at the model, saying the analysis showed it, without being able to reconstruct the reasoning yourself. That moment is worth noticing, because a decision you cannot explain is one you did not really make. The machine made it, and you carried it.

Being able to explain a decision is not a nicety. It is the proof that you understood it, and understanding is what lets you catch an error. If you can only point at the tool, you have no independent grasp of why the decision was right, which means you have no way to tell when it is wrong. You are trusting blindly and calling it deciding.

So treat the inability to explain as a warning. When you notice you cannot say why, beyond the model said so, you have found a decision that slipped out of your hands.

The fix is to insist on understanding before you act, at least on the calls that matter. Be able to explain the decision in your own words, without pointing at the tool. If you cannot, do the work until you can, or acknowledge that you are deferring rather than deciding. A decision you can explain is yours. A decision you can only attribute to the machine was the machine's, and you were just the one who clicked.
