---
title: "The Base-Rate Fix Is a Question"
date: 2026-02-13
pageDescription: "Ask how often this is true in general, before you act on a striking answer."
---
When an answer feels striking, one small question puts it back in proportion. How often is this true in general? That is the base rate, and asking for it is the cheapest error-correction you have.

A model flags a transaction as fraud. Impressive, until you ask how many transactions are actually fraud, and realize most flags will be false. It scores a candidate as exceptional. Encouraging, until you ask how many people it scores that way, and the score means less. The striking result needs the boring context to mean anything.

The reason this works is that dramatic answers get their drama by ignoring how rare the dramatic case is. Restore the rarity and the drama shrinks to its right size.

You do not need statistics for this. You need the habit of one question, asked before you act. How common is this thing, really? Then judge the answer against that; it is not glamorous, and it will save you from acting on a parade of confident flags that, taken in context, were mostly noise.
