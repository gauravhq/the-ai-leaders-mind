---
title: "AI in Customer Service"
date: 2026-02-14
pageDescription: "Automate the routine, but know which customer needs a person, and route them fast."
---
Customer service is where automation is most visible and most often resented. Tools can handle enormous volumes of routine questions well, and that is a genuine good, for the company and for customers who just want a fast answer. The failure is not automation itself. It is automation that traps the person who needs a human behind a wall that will not let them through.

The art is knowing which is which. A simple, common request is well served by a tool, quickly and at any hour. A frustrated, complex, or emotional situation needs a person, and the customer knows it, and making them fight through the automation to reach one turns a solvable problem into a grievance.

So the design that works is not maximum automation. It is automation for the routine paired with a fast, obvious path to a human for everything else. The sentiment the tool detects should be routing information, a signal to hand off, not a reason to keep the person in the loop longer.

Use the machine to handle scale and to free your people for the interactions that need them. Then make sure the handoff is easy, because the moment a customer needs a human and cannot reach one is the moment the efficiency you gained turns into the trust you lost, and trust is far more expensive to rebuild than a queue is to staff.
