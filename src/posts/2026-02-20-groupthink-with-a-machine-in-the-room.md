---
title: "Groupthink With a Machine in the Room"
date: 2026-02-20
pageDescription: "The model can become the authority no one in the meeting dares to question."
---
Groupthink is old. A room converges on a view, dissent feels costly, and the convergence gets mistaken for being right. A machine in the room can make it worse.

Once a model's answer is on the screen, it carries a strange kind of authority. It seems neutral, informed, above the politics of the room. Disagreeing with it can feel like arguing with the data itself. So people fall quiet, and the meeting agrees with the tool the way it used to agree with the most senior person present.

The danger is that the model's answer was one input, not a verdict, and now it is functioning as a verdict because no one wants to be the person who doubts it.

The fix is a leader who actively invites the challenge. Ask the room what the model might be missing. Ask for the strongest case against it. Make questioning the tool a normal, rewarded thing rather than a small act of courage. A machine that cannot be disagreed with is not helping the room think; it is just ending the conversation early.
