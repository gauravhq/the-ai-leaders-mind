---
title: "AI in Performance Reviews"
date: 2026-02-21
pageDescription: "A tool can summarize the year. It cannot understand the person."
---
Performance reviews are increasingly shaped by tools that aggregate data and even draft the assessment. Used carefully this helps. Used carelessly it turns a human judgment about a human being into a data exercise, and people can tell the difference between being understood and being scored.

The tool sees what was logged, measured, recorded. It does not see the context, the difficult project that failed for good reasons, the quiet person who held the team together in ways no metric captured, the growth that does not show up in the numbers yet. A review driven by the data alone rewards the legible and misses the real, and people learn to perform for the metrics rather than do the work.

There is also the matter of dignity. A review that reads as machine-generated tells the person exactly how much thought they got, which is to say not much.

So let the tool assemble the raw material, and keep the judgment and the delivery human. Bring the context the data missed. Write and deliver the review as a person who actually saw the year, not as a relay for a system that only saw its traces. The person deserves to be understood, not just measured, and understanding is the one thing the tool cannot supply.
