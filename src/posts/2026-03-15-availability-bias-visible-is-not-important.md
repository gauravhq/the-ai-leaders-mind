---
title: "Availability Bias: Mistaking Visible for Important"
date: 2026-03-15
pageDescription: "We treat what is easy to see as what matters most. The dashboard feeds the habit."
---
We treat what is easy to see as what matters most. The dashboard shows what it can count, and slowly the countable becomes the whole picture. That is availability bias, and AI systems are very good at feeding it.

A model surfaces the metrics it was given. Response time, conversion, churn. These are real, but they are not everything, and the things left off the screen do not stop existing. Morale, trust, the person who is about to quit, the customer too frustrated to fill in the survey. None of it fits neatly in a chart, so none of it competes for your attention.

The risk for a leader is managing the visible and losing the rest. You optimize the number on the screen and wonder why the room feels worse.

Once a week, ask a different question. What matters here that nothing is measuring? The answer is usually where the real problem is hiding, exactly because no dashboard is pointing at it.
