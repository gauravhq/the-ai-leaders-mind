---
title: "The Productivity Slide Mistake"
date: 2026-04-04
pageDescription: "Answering an emotional objection with a better efficiency argument makes it worse."
---
Leaders make this mistake constantly. Someone resists a new tool, clearly uneasy, and the leader responds with data. Look how much time it saves. Look at the numbers. The better the slide, the deeper the person digs in, and the leader cannot understand why the evidence is not working.

It is not working because it is answering a question no one asked. The resistance was emotional, a fear about place and value and identity, and it got met with a spreadsheet. To the person, that spreadsheet says the leader heard none of what actually worried them, and cares about the number more than about them.

So the productivity case, however airtight, lands as dismissal. It confirms the fear rather than easing it.

The move is to put the slide away until the fear has been heard. Name what the person is actually worried about, let them feel understood, and only then, if it is even still needed, talk about the benefits. Evidence persuades people who already feel safe. To people who feel threatened, it sounds like being told their feelings do not count. Safety first, numbers later, or the numbers make it worse.
