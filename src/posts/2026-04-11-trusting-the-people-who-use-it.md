---
title: "Trusting the People Who Use It"
date: 2026-04-11
pageDescription: "The most dangerous team is the one that has learned to stay quiet when the machine is wrong."
---
A tool is only as good as the judgment around it. The same model in two teams produces two different outcomes, because trust in the machine is inseparable from trust in the people holding it.

This is the corner leaders forget. They vet the vendor and audit the algorithm, then hand it to a team they have never taught to question it. The result is people who either rubber-stamp the output or quietly ignore it, and no way to tell which.

Trusting your people with AI is not hoping they get it right. It is building the conditions where getting it right is possible. They need to know what the tool is for, where it fails, and that raising a doubt will be rewarded rather than punished.

The most dangerous team is not the one that distrusts the machine. It is the one that has learned to stay quiet when the machine is wrong. If your people cannot safely say the model looks off, you do not have trust. You have silence wearing its coat.
