---
title: "Over-Trust Feels Like Relief"
date: 2026-05-09
pageDescription: "The more common failure is not doubting the tool. It is the relief of leaning on it."
---
When people worry about trusting machines, they usually picture blind faith, someone naively believing a bad answer. The real thing is quieter. It does not feel like faith. It feels like relief.

A hard decision is a weight. When a tool offers to carry it, setting the weight down feels good, and that good feeling does most of the work of over-trust. You are not thinking the model must be right. You are enjoying not having to decide, and calling it trust.

That is why over-trust is more common than under-trust. Doubt is effortful. Deferring is a rest. And the rest is seductive exactly when the decision is hardest, which is exactly when your judgment mattered most.

So notice the relief. When accepting an answer feels like a weight lifting, treat that as a flag, not a green light. Ask whether you are trusting the tool because it earned it, or because deferring was easier than deciding. The relief is real; it is just not evidence that the answer was right.
