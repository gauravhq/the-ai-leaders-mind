---
title: "AI in Forecasting"
date: 2026-05-15
pageDescription: "A confident forecast is still a guess about a future the model cannot see."
---
Forecasting is where models look most authoritative and are most quietly fallible. A tool produces a precise projection, demand, revenue, risk, and the precision makes it feel like knowledge. But a forecast is a guess about a future that has not happened, and no amount of precision changes that. The decimal places are confidence, not certainty.

The danger is treating the forecast as a fact and building on it as if the future were settled. The model extrapolates from the past, and the future departs from the past exactly when it matters most, in the shocks and shifts the historical data never contained. A forecast is most confident and least reliable precisely at the turning points where you most need to see them coming.

So hold forecasts as what they are, informed guesses with a range of error that the single clean number hides. Ask what the projection assumes, what would break it, how wrong it could be. Plan for the range, not the point.

Use the tool to forecast, because a disciplined guess beats a hunch. Just do not mistake its confidence for knowledge of the future. The number on the screen is a projection of the past forward, and the future's job is to be surprising. A leader who treats a forecast as a fact is well prepared for a future that behaves, and helpless in the one that actually arrives.
