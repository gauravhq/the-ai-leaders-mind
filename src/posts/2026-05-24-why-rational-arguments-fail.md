---
title: "Why Rational Arguments Fail"
date: 2026-05-24
pageDescription: "You cannot reason someone out of a fear you have not named."
---
Show someone the data, prove the tool works, and watch them nod and change nothing. It is one of the most frustrating moments in leading change, and it is completely predictable.

Resistance to AI is rarely a failure of information. It is a feeling, usually fear, and feelings do not yield to spreadsheets. When you meet an emotional objection with a rational argument, you are answering a question no one asked. The person hears that you have not understood them, so they dig in harder.

This is why the productivity case, however airtight, so often makes resistance worse. It signals that the leader cares about the number and not the person attached to it.

The move that works is slower and feels less like winning. You name the fear before you make the case. You let the person feel understood before you ask them to change. Only then does the evidence land, because now it is arriving as help rather than as pressure. Facts persuade people who already feel safe. Safety comes first, or the facts bounce off.
