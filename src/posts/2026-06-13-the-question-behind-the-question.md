---
title: "The Question Behind the Question"
date: 2026-06-13
pageDescription: "The model answers what you asked. Your job is to know what you actually needed."
---
A model is very good at answering the question you type; it is not good at noticing that you asked the wrong one. That gap is where a lot of confident, useless answers come from.

You ask how to cut costs, and it helps you cut costs, when the real problem was that the product was losing people. You ask which candidate scores highest, and it tells you, when the question you needed was what kind of person this role actually requires. The tool optimizes the target you gave it, even when the target was off.

So the human work moves upstream, to the framing. Before you trust an answer, check the question. Is this what I actually need to know, or just what was easy to ask? What would I be missing if this answer were perfect?

The machine will not do this for you. Framing the right question is judgment, and it is one of the parts of the job that gets more important, not less, as the answering gets automated.
