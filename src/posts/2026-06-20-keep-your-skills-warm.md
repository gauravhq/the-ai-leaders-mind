---
title: "Keep Your Skills Warm"
date: 2026-06-20
pageDescription: "A capability you never use is one you are quietly losing."
---
A skill is not a possession you keep once you have earned it. It is closer to a fire that needs tending. Stop using it and it cools, slowly at first, then past the point where you notice, until the day you reach for it and it is not there. The tools make this easy to let happen, because they are always willing to do the thing for you.

The dangerous part is that losing a skill feels like nothing while it happens. You are more productive, the work gets done, and the capability fades in the background with no alarm attached. You only discover the loss when you need the skill and the tool is gone, or wrong, and you have to stand on your own.

So keep the important skills warm on purpose. Make some decisions unaided. Do the estimate before you check it against the model. Solve the occasional problem the slow way, not because it is efficient but because it keeps the capability alive.

This is not nostalgia or inefficiency. It is insurance. The skills you tend are the ones that will be there in the moment the tools fail you, and that moment always comes eventually. A warm skill is ready. A cold one is a memory of something you used to be able to do.
