---
title: "Base Rates the Model Forgets"
date: 2026-07-04
pageDescription: "A confident answer can quietly ignore how rare a thing actually is."
---
One of the oldest errors in judgment is ignoring how common or rare a thing is to begin with. A test that flags a disease sounds alarming until you remember how few people have it, at which point most of the flags turn out to be false; that is the base rate, and it is easy to forget.

Models can forget it too, or rather, they can hand you an answer that steps right past it. A confident classification, a striking pattern, a sharp prediction, all can look convincing while quietly assuming the thing in question is more common than it is.

The correction is a plain question. How often is this true in general? If the model flags something as fraud, or a candidate as a star, or a deal as a risk, ask what the underlying rate is before you act on the flag.

Base rates are unglamorous and they save you constantly. When an answer feels dramatic, the base rate is often the boring fact that puts the drama back in proportion.
