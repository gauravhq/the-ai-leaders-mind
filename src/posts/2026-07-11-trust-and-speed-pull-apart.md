---
title: "Trust and Speed Pull Apart"
date: 2026-07-11
pageDescription: "The faster you can act on a tool, the more tempting it is to skip the trust question."
---
Speed and trust work against each other in a quiet way. The faster a tool lets you act, the less time there is to ask whether you should, and the temptation is to let the speed answer the trust question by default. It gave you an answer quickly, so you use it quickly, and the deciding whether to rely on it never happens.

This is fine on small, reversible calls, where the cost of being wrong is low and speed is the whole point. It is dangerous on the calls that matter, where the same fast interface carries a decision that deserved a real trust check and did not get one.

The tool will not slow down for the important ones. It moves at the same pace regardless of stakes. So the pause has to come from you, and it has to come precisely where speed is most seductive, on the confident, fast answer to a hard question.

Let speed win where being wrong is cheap. Insert the trust question yourself where being wrong is expensive. The interface will never draw that line for you, because to the machine every decision is just the next quick answer.
