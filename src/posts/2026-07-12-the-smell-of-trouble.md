Long before an ethical problem shows up in a report, it gives off a smell. Leaders who avoid disasters are usually the ones who learned to notice it early.

The signs are quiet. A decision no one wants to put their name to. An explanation that only works if you do not look too closely. A metric doing suspiciously well while the people it measures seem worse off. A quiet reluctance to show the affected person how the call was made. None of these prove wrongdoing. All of them are worth a second look.

The trouble with AI systems is that they can make the smell fainter. The output is clean, the interface is calm, and the discomfort gets buried under a layer of technical polish.

So trust the discomfort. That flicker of not wanting to explain the decision to the person it lands on is information. It is your ethical sense working faster than your reasoning. The move is not to suppress it and proceed. It is to stop and ask what your gut noticed before your logic caught up. The smell is usually right.
