---
title: "Dignity in an Automated Decision"
date: 2026-08-21
pageDescription: "How you tell someone can matter as much as what you decided."
---
When a decision affects a person, how they learn it matters almost as much as the decision itself. A rejection delivered with care and a rejection delivered by a cold automated message can carry the same outcome and land completely differently, because one preserved the person's dignity and the other did not.

As more decisions get made with machines, this becomes easy to forget. The system produces an outcome, a message goes out, and no one quite owns the moment of telling. The person on the receiving end feels that absence. They were not turned down by anyone. They were processed by something, and it stings in a way a human no would not.

Dignity costs a little effort and buys a lot of trust. A decision explained by a real person, with honesty about how it was reached and room for the person to respond, respects them even when the answer is no.

So keep a human in the moment of delivery, especially for the decisions that hurt. The machine can help you decide. It should not be the thing that tells someone their livelihood, or their hope, just changed.
