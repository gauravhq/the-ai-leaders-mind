---
title: "The Early Adopter Problem"
date: 2026-10-24
pageDescription: "The people who love the tool can scale its mistakes as fast as its benefits."
---
Enthusiasm has a blind spot. The people who adopt a new tool eagerly, who love what it can do, are exactly the people least likely to notice where it goes wrong. Their excitement is real and useful, and it can also carry a tool's errors across the organization at speed.

An early adopter trusts the tool because it has impressed them, and that trust makes them less likely to check it. They become advocates, spreading both the genuine value and the quiet failure modes with equal energy, because they are not looking for the failures. They are looking for the wins.

This is not a reason to distrust your enthusiasts. It is a reason to pair their energy with someone else's skepticism. The people dragging their feet, the ones asking annoying questions about reliability, are often seeing something real that the enthusiasts have talked themselves past.

So value both. Let the early adopters find the possibilities. Let the skeptics find the boundaries. A rollout driven only by enthusiasm scales the mistakes as fast as the benefits, because the people moving fastest are the ones least inclined to look down.
