---
title: "Consent and the Machine"
date: 2026-10-31
pageDescription: "People affected by an automated decision deserve to know one is being made."
---
There is a quiet ethical question that automated systems make easy to skip. Do the people affected by a decision know that a machine is making it, or shaping it? Often they do not, and the not-knowing is its own kind of wrong, separate from whether the decision was good.

People have a reasonable interest in understanding how consequential decisions about them are made. When a model decides who gets the loan, the interview, the flag, the price, and no one is told, something has been taken without asking. The person cannot question a process they do not know exists, cannot contest an error they cannot see, cannot even know to be concerned.

This is not an argument against using the tools. It is an argument for honesty about using them. Where a machine meaningfully shapes decisions about people, those people generally deserve to know, and to have some path to question or appeal.

So ask, for the decisions your tools touch, whether the affected people know and whether they have any recourse. Transparency here is not just good ethics. It is what keeps the whole system honest, because a process that has to be explainable to the people it affects is a process that gets built with more care.
