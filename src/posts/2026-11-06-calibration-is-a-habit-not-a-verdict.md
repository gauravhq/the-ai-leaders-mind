---
title: "Calibration Is a Habit, Not a Verdict"
date: 2026-11-06
pageDescription: "You do not calibrate trust once. You keep adjusting it as the evidence comes in."
---
People treat trust in a tool as a verdict to reach and then file. Decide once whether it is reliable, and act on that forever. But a tool is not fixed, your tasks are not fixed, and a single verdict goes stale the moment you make it.

Calibration is a habit, not a conclusion. It means watching how the tool performs, task by task, and adjusting your reliance as the evidence accumulates. Where it keeps proving reliable, trust it more. Where it stumbles, trust it less. And keep doing this, because both the tool and the work keep changing.

The leaders who work best with machines are not the ones who decided long ago how much to trust them. They are the ones still paying attention, still updating, still treating trust as something alive rather than settled.

So do not look for the verdict. Look for the practice. Keep a running, honest sense of where this tool earns reliance and where it does not, and let that sense move. A verdict is comfortable and wrong. A habit of calibration is more work and the only thing that keeps trust matched to reality.
