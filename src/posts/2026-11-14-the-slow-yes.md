---
title: "The Slow Yes"
date: 2026-11-14
pageDescription: "A grudging early adoption that holds beats an enthusiastic one that collapses."
---
There are two ways a change can be accepted, and the fast one is often the weaker one. A quick, enthusiastic yes feels like success and frequently is not, because it skipped the working-through that makes acceptance hold. A slow yes, arrived at through real doubt and real questions, tends to last.

People who adopt something instantly sometimes have not engaged with it deeply enough to stay committed when it gets hard. The first real friction, and the enthusiasm collapses, because it was never built on much. People who take longer, who push back and test and worry, are doing the work that makes their eventual yes durable.

So do not mistake speed of acceptance for depth of it, and do not be discouraged by resistance that is thoughtful. The person asking hard questions is often on their way to a stronger commitment than the one who cheered on day one.

Let the yes be slow when it needs to be. Answer the questions, sit with the doubts, do not rush people past their own working-through. A slow yes that holds under pressure is worth far more than a fast one that evaporates the first time the tool disappoints.
