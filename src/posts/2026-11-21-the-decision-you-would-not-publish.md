---
title: "The Decision You Would Not Publish"
date: 2026-11-21
pageDescription: "If you would be uncomfortable seeing it on the front page, look again."
---
There is a quick ethical gut-check for a decision made with a machine. Imagine it described plainly on the front page of a newspaper, or explained in open court, or read aloud to the people it affects. If that thought makes you uncomfortable, the discomfort is information.

The test works because harm hides in abstraction, and publicity strips the abstraction away. A decision that looks fine as an output on a dashboard can look very different described in plain language to an audience, and the difference is often exactly the part your metrics were not capturing. The flinch you feel at the thought of it being public is your own moral sense noticing what the numbers missed.

This is not about fear of getting caught. It is about using imagined transparency to see your decision clearly. The things we would not want seen are usually the things we already half know are wrong.

So when a decision sits uneasily, run it through the front page. Could you stand behind this if everyone could see it and how it was made? If the honest answer is no, do not rely on the fact that no one is looking. Look yourself, and fix what the imagined light revealed.
