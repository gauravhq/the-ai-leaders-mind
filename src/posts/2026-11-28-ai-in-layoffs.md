---
title: "AI in Layoffs"
date: 2026-11-28
pageDescription: "A decision this heavy must never be one a person can hide from behind a model."
---
Few decisions carry more human weight than deciding who loses their job, and few are more dangerous to hand to a machine. A model can identify candidates for a layoff by whatever criteria it is given. The moment it is allowed to make or launder the decision, something has gone badly wrong, because a decision this heavy must have a human owner who cannot hide behind it.

The trap is exactly the hiding. A layoff decided by a model feels less personal, less like anyone's fault, and that relief is the danger. A choice that should sit heavily on a person becomes an output, and the weight that would have made someone deliberate carefully lifts away. People get cut by a system, and no one quite decided it.

If a tool is involved at all, its role is to inform, never to own. A person decides, in their own name, having felt the full weight. And a person delivers it, face to face, with honesty and care, because how someone learns they have lost their livelihood matters enormously.

So keep this decision as human as any decision gets. The machine can provide information. It cannot carry the responsibility, and it must never become the place a leader goes to avoid carrying it themselves. On a call this heavy, the willingness to own it fully is most of what makes it survivable for everyone involved.
