---
title: "Psychological Safety Around AI"
date: 2026-12-26
pageDescription: "If people cannot safely say the model looks wrong, you do not have trust. You have silence."
---
The most dangerous team is not the one that distrusts its tools; it is the one that has quietly learned to stay quiet. When people do not feel safe questioning the model, its errors sail through unchallenged, and you lose the one defense you had.

Psychological safety is what lets a junior person say the output looks off, or a specialist flag that the recommendation does not fit the case in front of them. Without it, everyone defers, the tool becomes unquestionable, and the room agrees with a machine it privately doubts.

Building that safety is a leader's job, and it is built by how you react. When someone challenges the tool and turns out to be right, that has to be visibly rewarded. When they challenge it and are wrong, that has to be fine too, or no one will risk it next time.

A team that can freely say the machine looks wrong catches problems early. A team that has learned to stay silent finds out later, in the failure. Safety is not softness. It is the condition for catching the errors a confident tool will otherwise hide.
