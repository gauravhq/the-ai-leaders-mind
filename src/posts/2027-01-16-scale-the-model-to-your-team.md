---
title: "Scale the Model to Your Team"
date: 2027-01-16
pageDescription: "The same decision layers work for a team, if you make the roles explicit."
---
The layers of a good decision are not just for you alone. They scale to a team, and doing so deliberately turns a private discipline into a shared one. The machine's input, the independent reads, the gap, the human check, the owned call, each has a place in how a group decides, if you make the roles explicit.

The most common failure in team decisions with a tool is that everyone sees the model's output first and anchors to it, and the independent reads never happen. So the adaptation is to protect the second layer at the group level. Have people form and even write their own views before the model's answer is discussed, so the room brings genuinely independent reads rather than a chorus of agreement with the screen.

Then the gap becomes a group signal. Where do people, and the model, disagree, and why? That is where the discussion should concentrate. And the owned call stays with a named person, not the committee, so responsibility does not dissolve into consensus.

So scale the method on purpose. Independent reads first, gaps explored openly, a clear owner at the end. A team that runs this stays a group of thinkers rather than a group deferring together to a tool, which is what teams quietly become when no one protects the independent view.
