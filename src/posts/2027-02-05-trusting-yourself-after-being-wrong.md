---
title: "Trusting Yourself After Being Wrong"
date: 2027-02-05
pageDescription: "One bad call against the model can quietly retire your own judgment. Do not let it."
---
There is a trap worth naming. You override the tool, you turn out to be wrong, and something in you concludes you should stop overriding it. One mistake against the machine, and your own judgment goes on probation, then quietly into retirement.

It is an understandable reaction and a bad lesson. Being wrong once does not mean the tool is always right and you are always wrong. It means you were wrong once, which is true of every person and every model that has ever existed. The machine gets things wrong too, constantly, and it does not respond by deferring to you forever.

If you let a single bad override end your willingness to form your own view, you have handed the tool a permanent win it did not earn. And you will need your judgment again, on the day the machine is the one that is confidently wrong, and by then it will be out of practice.

So take the lesson without taking the whole self-trust with it. You were wrong on that call. Learn why. Then keep forming your own view, because a judgment that quits after one loss was never going to be there when it counted.
