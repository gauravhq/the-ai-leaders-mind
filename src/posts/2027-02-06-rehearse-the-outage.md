---
title: "Rehearse the Outage"
date: 2027-02-06
pageDescription: "Imagine the tools gone before they are, so you are not helpless when they are."
---
The Stoics practiced imagining loss before it came, not to be gloomy but to be ready. Applied to your tools, it is a useful drill. Picture, on an ordinary day, that the systems you rely on are suddenly gone. The model is down, the dashboard is dark, the automation has failed. What can you still do?

The value of asking this before it happens is that the answer, today, tells you where you have quietly become dependent. If the imagined outage leaves you helpless, you have outsourced something you cannot afford to lose, and you have found this out cheaply, in imagination, instead of expensively, in a real failure.

The drill also builds the calm that comes from having thought it through. A leader who has already pictured the systems failing, and knows roughly what they would do, does not panic when it happens. They have rehearsed.

So rehearse the outage on purpose. Not with dread, but as preparation. What decisions could you still make? What skills are still yours without the tools? The gaps you find are your homework, the places to keep a human capability alive. Better to meet them in a rehearsal than in the real thing, with everyone watching and the tools genuinely gone.
