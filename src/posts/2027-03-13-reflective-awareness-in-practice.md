---
title: "Reflective Awareness in Practice"
date: 2027-03-13
pageDescription: "Watching your own thinking as it happens is the skill under all the others."
---
Of all the capacities that matter when working with a machine, one sits underneath the rest. The ability to watch your own thinking while you are doing it. Without it, every other skill is theoretical, because you cannot correct a bias you cannot see in yourself in the moment it is happening.

Reflective awareness is noticing the pull to defer before you defer, catching the small relief of accepting an answer, feeling yourself anchor before the anchor sets. It is the difference between being run by your reflexes and being able to interrupt them. And it is a skill, which means it can be built.

You build it in small reps. A moment at the end of a decision, asking how you actually reached it. A habit of naming a reflex as it fires, quietly, to yourself. It feels awkward at first, like narrating your own mind, and then it becomes a background hum that catches things you used to miss.

So practice watching yourself decide, not just deciding. The tools are designed to be smooth, to invite you to stop thinking and accept, and the only defense against that smoothness is the part of you that can notice it happening. Reflective awareness is that part, and everything else in leading well with machines is built on top of it.
