---
title: "The Fear of Losing Control"
date: 2027-03-13
pageDescription: "Handing a decision to a system you cannot fully see is its own kind of dread."
---
Some resistance to AI is about control. A person built their competence on understanding their work, seeing how a decision was reached, being able to stand behind it. A tool that produces an answer from a process they cannot fully see takes that away, and the loss feels like dread, even when the answer is good.

This is not stubbornness. It is the reasonable discomfort of being asked to rely on something you cannot inspect. For someone who took pride in knowing why, a black box that just says the answer is a real loss, not an imagined one.

You cannot fully remove this fear, because the loss is partly real. But you can shrink it by restoring what control you can. Give people visibility into how the tool works, where it is reliable, where it fails. Let them keep the final call rather than being overridden by the system. Show them the parts they still own.

Control that is respected can be shared. Control that is taken is resisted. The person afraid of losing control is telling you they want to stay a participant in their own decisions, not a spectator, and that is a request worth honoring.
