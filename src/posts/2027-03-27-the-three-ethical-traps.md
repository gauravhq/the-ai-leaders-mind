---
title: "The Three Ethical Traps"
date: 2027-03-27
pageDescription: "The moment a decision belongs to no one is the moment ethics leaves the room."
---
Most bad ethical decisions with AI are not made by bad people. They are made by good people who fell into one of three traps without noticing.

The first is hiding behind the machine. The model decided, so no one feels responsible, and a choice that would trouble any single person passes because it belongs to no one. The second is treating a number as if it settled the ethics. The system is seventy percent accurate, so we proceed, as though accuracy answered the question of who the other thirty percent are. The third is speed. The tool makes the decision so easy and so fast that the moment where you might have paused to think never arrives.

Each trap works by removing the human from the loop while leaving the consequences firmly in the human world.

The way out of all three is the same. Put a person back where the buck stops. Someone has to own the decision, in their own name, as if the machine were only advising. The moment a choice belongs to no one is the moment ethics quietly leaves the room.
