---
title: "Hold Your Identity Lightly"
date: 2027-04-03
pageDescription: "Tie your sense of self to the ability to adapt, not to any skill the machine might copy."
---
There is a way of holding your professional identity that makes change threatening, and a way that makes it survivable. If your sense of who you are is tied to a specific skill, then anything that automates that skill feels like an attack on your self, and you will resist it even when resistance costs you. If you hold your identity more lightly, change becomes something you can move through.

This is not about caring less. It is about locating your identity somewhere the machine cannot reach. Not in the particular thing you are good at today, which the tools may well come for, but in your ability to learn, adapt, and bring judgment to whatever comes next. That version of you is not threatened by automation, because it is not staked on any single capability.

The people who struggle most with these transitions are often the ones whose identity fused with a skill the machine now imitates. The skill erodes and they feel erased, because they made the skill into who they are.

So hold it lightly. Be good at things without becoming them. Let your sense of worth rest on the deeper, more durable capacities, the judgment and the adaptability, rather than on the specific task of the moment. An identity held lightly can update as the world changes. One held tightly shatters the first time the world moves.
