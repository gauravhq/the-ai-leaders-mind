---
title: "The Number Is Not the Moral Answer"
date: 2027-04-17
pageDescription: "A metric can tell you what is efficient. It cannot tell you what is right."
---
A model can optimize any number you give it, and it will, with a focus no human can match. What it cannot do is tell you whether the number was the right thing to chase. That judgment is not technical, and handing it to the machine is a category error that causes real harm.

The confusion is easy to fall into. The tool produces a clear, quantified answer, and clarity feels like correctness. But the number only tells you what is efficient toward the target you set. Whether that target was worth pursuing, whether the tradeoffs are acceptable, whether someone is being harmed in the pursuit, none of that is in the number.

So keep the moral question separate from the metric. Efficiency, accuracy, optimization, these are means. What they should serve is a human judgment about what matters, and that judgment cannot be delegated to the thing doing the optimizing.

When a decision reduces to a number, ask what the number left out. The metric is a tool for reaching a goal. It is never a substitute for deciding whether the goal was right, and a leader who lets it become one has quietly stopped doing the part of the job that most needed a person.
