---
title: "Meet Fear With Understanding First"
date: 2027-04-24
pageDescription: "People change when they feel understood, not when they are argued into it."
---
The order matters more than the argument. People move through fear when they first feel understood, and almost never when they are simply argued into a new position. Understanding is not a nicety you add after making your case. It is the thing that makes the case hearable at all.

When someone is afraid and you lead with reasons, they hear that you are trying to override them, and they brace. When you lead with understanding, naming what they feel and showing you take it seriously, the bracing eases, and only then is there room for anything you want to say next.

This is slower and feels less like winning, which is why leaders skip it. But the fast version, where you overwhelm the fear with logic, does not actually work. It produces compliance at best and quiet sabotage at worst, because the fear went underground rather than away.

So spend the time up front. Understand before you persuade. Let the person feel met before you ask them to move. The argument you were going to make will land far better on ground that has been made safe first, and often you will find you need much less of it than you thought.
