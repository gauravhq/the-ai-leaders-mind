---
title: "Who the Model Gets Wrong"
date: 2027-04-24
pageDescription: "Every system has errors. The ethical question is who absorbs them."
---
Every model gets some things wrong. That is not a scandal, it is a fact of any system that makes predictions. The ethical question is not whether it errs but who absorbs the errors, and that question gets asked far too rarely.

Errors are almost never spread evenly. A tool that works well for the common case can fail systematically for a smaller group, the people underrepresented in its data, the cases that do not fit the pattern. The overall accuracy looks fine, and a specific population quietly bears most of the harm.

So look past the average to the distribution. Where does this tool fail, and who is standing there when it does? A system that is ninety percent accurate overall but wrong most of the time for one group is not ninety percent fair. It has concentrated its damage, and the average is hiding it.

The people a model gets wrong are often the ones with the least power to object, which is exactly why someone with power has to ask on their behalf. Knowing who absorbs the errors is the difference between deploying a tool responsibly and scaling a harm you never bothered to look for.
