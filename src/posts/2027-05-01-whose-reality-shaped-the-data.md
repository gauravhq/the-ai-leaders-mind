---
title: "Whose Reality Shaped the Data"
date: 2027-05-01
pageDescription: "A model learns from a slice of the world. Ask whose slice it was."
---
Every model is a compression of the data it learned from, and that data is a slice of the world, not the whole of it. Whose slice matters enormously, because whatever the data over-represented, the model treats as normal, and whatever it left out, the model treats as not existing.

This is where a lot of quiet unfairness enters. If the data came mostly from one kind of person, place, or situation, the model works best for them and worst for everyone else, and it will not tell you this. It will answer just as confidently about the cases it barely saw as the ones it saw constantly.

So ask, for any tool you rely on, whose reality shaped it. What was over-represented in the data, and what was missing? The people or cases that were absent from the training are the ones the model will handle worst, and they are often the ones with the least power to make the failure visible.

You do not need to be a data scientist to ask the question. Whose world taught this tool, and whose world did it never see? The answer tells you where to trust it and, more importantly, where to check it hardest, because the gaps in the data become the gaps in the fairness.
