---
title: "Could You Explain It to Their Face"
date: 2027-05-15
pageDescription: "The plainest ethics test is whether you could justify the decision to the person it lands on."
---
There is a test for an automated decision that cuts through most of the complexity. Could you explain it, honestly, to the face of the person it affects? Not in jargon, not by pointing at the model, but in plain words that a reasonable person would accept as fair.

If you could, the decision is probably sound. If you find yourself reaching for the algorithm as a shield, or hoping the person never asks, that discomfort is telling you something the metrics will not. Your sense that you could not look them in the eye and justify it is a moral signal, and it is usually right.

This test works because it puts the human back at the center. A decision becomes abstract when it is just an output, and abstraction is where harm hides. Imagining the actual person, and imagining explaining yourself to them, restores the weight that the interface removed.

So before you let a consequential automated decision stand, run it. Picture the person on the other end. Could you tell them how this was decided and have it land as fair? If the honest answer is no, the decision needs more work, no matter what the accuracy score says.
