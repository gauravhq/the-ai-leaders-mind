---
title: "The Friction You Keep on Purpose"
date: 2027-07-03
pageDescription: "A little deliberate difficulty is how you stay capable in an easy world."
---
Tools remove friction, and mostly that is good. But total frictionlessness has a cost, because friction is often where the learning and the keeping-sharp happen. So the skill is not to remove all of it. It is to keep a little, on purpose, in the places that matter.

When every calculation is instant, every draft is generated, every answer is handed to you, the effort that used to build and maintain your capability disappears along with the inconvenience. You gain speed and lose the workout. And the workout was doing something you could not see, keeping you able.

Deliberate friction is choosing to do some things the harder way, not out of principle but out of self-interest. The occasional unaided decision, the draft you write yourself, the problem you sit with before reaching for the tool. Small frictions, chosen, that keep the muscles they exercise from wasting.

This runs against every instinct the tools train into you, which all point toward less effort, always. That is exactly why it has to be deliberate. Nothing in the system will insert this friction for you. Convenience is the default, and quiet decline is the price. A little friction, kept on purpose, is how you keep paying it down.
