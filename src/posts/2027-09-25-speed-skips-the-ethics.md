---
title: "Speed Skips the Ethics"
date: 2027-09-25
pageDescription: "The pause where you might have thought about right and wrong is the first casualty of a fast tool."
---
Ethics needs a pause, and speed is the thing that removes it. A tool that makes a decision easy and instant also makes the moment of reflection disappear, because the reflection was living in the gap between considering a choice and making it, and the tool closed the gap.

This is how good people make bad calls with machines. Not through malice, but through pace. The decision is produced so quickly and smoothly that the instant where someone might have asked whether it was right simply never arrives. There was no friction, so there was no thought.

The danger concentrates on exactly the wrong decisions. The ones that touch a person's livelihood, freedom, or dignity are the ones that most need a pause, and a fast confident system will wave them through at the same speed as everything else.

So put the pause back where it matters, on purpose. For decisions with real human stakes, insert a deliberate stop, a question, a moment to consider the ethics the speed would otherwise skip. Most calls do not need it. The ones that do will not get it unless you build it in, because the tool is always ready to move on before you have had a chance to think.
