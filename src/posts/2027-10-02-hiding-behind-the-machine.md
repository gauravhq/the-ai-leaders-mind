---
title: "Hiding Behind the Machine"
date: 2027-10-02
pageDescription: "When the model decided, no one feels responsible, and that is the trap."
---
There is a comfortable place to hide when a hard decision goes wrong, and the machine provides it. The model decided, or the model recommended it, and suddenly no one in the room feels fully responsible. The choice belonged to the system, and systems do not feel guilt.

This is one of the most seductive ethical traps, because it works without anyone deciding to be dishonest. A decision that would trouble any single person, made in their own name, passes easily when it belongs to no one. The machine launders the discomfort away, and a choice gets made that a person, owning it, would have refused.

The tell is the phrase itself. The model said so. When that becomes the justification, responsibility has quietly evaporated, and with it the care that responsibility brings.

The fix is to refuse the hiding place. Insist that a person owns every consequential decision in their own name, as if the tool had only advised them. Not the algorithm, not the process, not a vague collective. A specific human who will answer for it. The machine can inform the decision. It cannot absorb the responsibility, and the moment you let it seem to, you have lost the thing that keeps decisions honest.
