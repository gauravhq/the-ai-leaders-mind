---
title: "Borrowed Calm"
date: 2027-10-08
pageDescription: "A team under pressure draws its steadiness from you. Have some to lend."
---
People under pressure borrow their composure from whoever seems to have some. In a tense moment, a room will unconsciously calibrate to the calmest credible person in it, and if that is you, your steadiness becomes a resource the whole team draws on. This is a real and slightly heavy responsibility.

It means your inner state is not private during a crisis. It leaks, and people take from it. A leader who is quietly falling apart drains the room even while saying reassuring words, because people feel the state underneath the words. A leader who is genuinely steady lends that steadiness out, and a team can run on borrowed calm long enough to get through the hard part.

The catch is that you cannot lend what you do not have. Borrowed calm requires you to actually build some, through whatever practices keep you grounded, so there is something real for people to draw on rather than a performance that cracks.

So tend your own steadiness, partly for the team's sake. In the moments that matter, they will reach for it whether you offer it or not. Having genuine calm to lend, when the pressure is real and everyone is looking to you, is one of the quieter things a leader is actually for.
