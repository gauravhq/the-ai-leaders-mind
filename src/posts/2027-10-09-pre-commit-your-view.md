---
title: "Pre-Commit Your View"
date: 2027-10-09
pageDescription: "Write down what you think before the model speaks, so you can catch yourself later."
---
A small technique protects your judgment from the tool. Before you consult the model on a decision that matters, write down what you think and why. Just a few lines, committed before the machine speaks. Then look at the model's answer.

The value shows up in two ways. First, it forces you to actually form an independent view, the second layer of any good decision, rather than skipping straight to the tool and calling your agreement a decision. Second, it lets you catch yourself, because once the model's confident answer is in front of you, it is very easy to convince yourself you thought that all along. A written pre-commitment makes that quiet self-revision impossible.

You can see, in your own handwriting, where you actually stood before the machine influenced you. Sometimes the model changes your mind for good reasons, and that is fine. What you want to avoid is being moved without noticing, drifting toward the tool's answer while believing you reasoned your way there.

So pre-commit. A few honest lines before you look. It keeps you in the decision, and it gives you an honest record of what the tool did to your thinking, which is the only way to learn whether to trust it more or less next time.
