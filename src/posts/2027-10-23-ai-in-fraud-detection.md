---
title: "AI in Fraud Detection"
date: 2027-10-23
pageDescription: "A flag is a suspicion, not a verdict, and treating it as a verdict harms the innocent."
---
Fraud detection is a place where models genuinely help, and where forgetting their limits does real damage. A tool can flag suspicious activity far faster and more broadly than any person. But a flag is a suspicion, not a verdict, and the harm comes when it gets treated as proof.

Remember the base rate. Most transactions are not fraud, so even a good model produces many false flags, and behind each false flag is a real person wrongly suspected, their card frozen, their account locked, their legitimate activity treated as crime. The model's confidence does not change the fact that most of its alarms, in absolute terms, may be false.

So the design question is what happens after a flag. If a flag automatically punishes, the innocent get caught constantly. If a flag triggers a human review with a low-friction way for the wrongly-flagged to clear themselves, the tool helps without harming.

Use the model for what it is good at, casting a wide net for suspicious patterns. Keep a human judgment between the flag and the consequence, especially the consequences that fall on people. A flag is the beginning of a question, not the end of one, and a system that forgets this turns a useful tool into a machine for punishing the innocent at scale.
