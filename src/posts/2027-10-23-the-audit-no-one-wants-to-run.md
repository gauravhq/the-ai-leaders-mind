---
title: "The Audit No One Wants to Run"
date: 2027-10-23
pageDescription: "The check that might reveal your tool is causing harm is the one most worth doing."
---
There is a specific audit that organizations avoid, and the avoidance is telling. It is the honest look at whether a tool you rely on is quietly causing harm, failing a group, encoding a bias, producing outcomes you would not want to defend. No one wants to run it, because it might find something, and finding something means having to act.

The reluctance is human and dangerous. A tool that is working, by the metrics everyone watches, feels like a success to leave alone. Looking harder risks turning a success into a problem, and there is always a reason to do it later.

But the harm does not wait for you to be ready to look. It accumulates while the audit is postponed, concentrated on the people least able to make you notice. The check you are avoiding is usually the one most worth doing, precisely because avoiding it is so comfortable.

So run it, especially when you would rather not. Ask who your tools are failing, where the errors land, what you would find if you looked at the outcomes for the people the metrics do not foreground. The audit no one wants to run is where the real risks live, and the willingness to run it anyway is a large part of what responsible use of these tools actually means.
