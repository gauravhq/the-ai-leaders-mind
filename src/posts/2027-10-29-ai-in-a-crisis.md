---
title: "AI in a Crisis"
date: 2027-10-29
pageDescription: "When everything is failing, judgment and presence matter more, not less."
---
A crisis is the ultimate test of the balance between tool and judgment, because a crisis is exactly when the tools are most likely to fail and the stakes are highest. The situation is novel, the data is incomplete or wrong, and the model, trained on normal conditions, is least reliable precisely when you most need to be right.

This is why the skills that seem optional in calm times matter most here. The judgment you kept sharp, the ability to decide without the dashboard, the steadiness you built, the trust you earned with your people. In a crisis, these are what you actually run on, because the tools that carried you through the ordinary days may be exactly what has broken.

So a crisis rewards the leader who never fully outsourced their judgment and punishes the one who did. If your capability lived entirely in the systems, and the systems are down or wrong, you are helpless at the worst possible moment.

Use tools in a crisis where they still help, but lean on the human capabilities you kept alive, the judgment, the presence, the trust, the calm. This is the moment all the quiet work pays off or fails to. The leader who stayed capable, steady, and present without the machine is the one a team can follow through the worst of it, and that leader is built long before the crisis, in all the ordinary days of refusing to let the tools do the thinking entirely.
