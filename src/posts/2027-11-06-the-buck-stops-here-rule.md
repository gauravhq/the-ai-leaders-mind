---
title: "The Buck-Stops-Here Rule"
date: 2027-11-06
pageDescription: "For every consequential automated decision, name the person it stops with."
---
This rule is simple enough to actually use. For every decision your tools make or shape that carries real weight, name the person it stops with. Not a team, not a process, not the vendor, not the algorithm. One specific human who owns the outcome and will answer for it.

The rule works because it fixes the failure that automation invites, the quiet spreading of responsibility until no one holds it. When a decision has a named owner, that person reviews it differently. They question the tool more honestly, they do not hide behind it, they carry the weight, because the weight is explicitly theirs. Diffuse responsibility produces diffuse care. A named owner produces attention.

And it clarifies things before anything goes wrong, not after. The aim is not to have someone to blame later. It is to have someone genuinely responsible now, whose ownership pulls the decision toward more care while it is being made.

So apply it. Before a consequential automated decision goes live, ask who the buck stops with, and make sure that person knows it stops with them. If the answer is unclear, that is a problem to fix before the decision, not a question to scramble over after it has already caused harm.
