---
title: "Start With the Machine's Input"
date: 2027-12-04
pageDescription: "The model's answer is the first layer of a good decision, not the whole of it."
---
A good decision with a machine has a shape, and the first layer is the machine's input. What does the model say, and how sure is it? This is where the tool belongs, at the start, as one voice offering its read, not at the end delivering a verdict.

Treating the model's output as the first layer rather than the final answer changes everything downstream. It becomes something to weigh rather than something to accept, a contribution to the decision rather than a replacement for it. You take it seriously without letting it be the last word, because you know three more layers are coming.

The mistake is collapsing the whole decision into this one layer, letting the model's input be the input, the analysis, and the conclusion all at once. That is not using the tool. It is deferring to it, dressed up as efficiency.

So take the machine's input first, and take it as exactly that. What is the recommendation, and how confident is it? Note it, weigh it, and then keep going, because a decision that stops at the machine's input skipped the layers where the human judgment was supposed to live. The tool speaks first. It should not speak last.
