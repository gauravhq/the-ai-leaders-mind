---
title: "AI in Promotion Decisions"
date: 2027-12-25
pageDescription: "Who rises is a judgment about potential, and potential is what the data shows least."
---
Promotion decisions are increasingly informed by tools that score performance and readiness, and they are a place where the data is especially likely to mislead. Promotion is a judgment about potential, about who will do well in a role they have not held yet, and potential is close to the hardest thing for a model to see, because the data only records the past.

The tool can tell you who performed well in their current job. It cannot reliably tell you who will grow into the next one, and those are different questions. The person who excelled at the old role sometimes fails at the new one, and the person whose potential outran their current metrics sometimes becomes your best leader. A model optimizing on past performance will systematically favor the first over the second.

There is also the quiet reproduction of old patterns. If the tool learned from who was promoted before, it inherits whatever bias shaped those choices, and hands it back as objective.

So use the data as one input and keep the judgment human, especially about potential. Ask who the numbers might be overlooking, whose growth does not yet show in the metrics, whether the model is just favoring the people who look like past winners. Who rises in an organization shapes its future, and that is far too important, and too much about potential, to hand to a tool that can only see the past.
