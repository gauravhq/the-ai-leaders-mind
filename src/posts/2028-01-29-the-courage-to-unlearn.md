---
title: "The Courage to Unlearn"
date: 2028-01-29
pageDescription: "Sometimes the hardest part of adapting is letting go of what used to work."
---
We talk about learning new things as if the only difficulty were acquiring them. Often the harder part is letting go of the old thing, the approach that used to work, the expertise you built, the way you were sure was right. Unlearning takes a kind of courage that learning does not, because it means admitting that something you were good at no longer serves.

This is especially hard for experienced people, which is a quiet irony of every technological shift. The more you have invested in a way of doing things, the more it costs to release it, and so the most skilled are sometimes the slowest to adapt, holding onto a mastery that the world has moved past.

Unlearning does not mean the old knowledge was worthless. It means being willing to set it down when it stops fitting, rather than forcing the new situation to match your existing expertise.

So practice the harder half of adaptation. When a tool or a change makes an old approach obsolete, notice the grip you have on it and be willing to loosen it. The courage to unlearn, to let go of what made you good in order to become good at what comes next, is rarer than the willingness to learn, and in a fast-changing field it is often the thing that separates the people who keep growing from the ones who quietly get left behind.
