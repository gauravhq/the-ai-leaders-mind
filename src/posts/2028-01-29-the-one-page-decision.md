---
title: "The One-Page Decision"
date: 2028-01-29
pageDescription: "If you cannot explain the call in a page, you do not yet understand it."
---
There is a discipline in forcing a decision onto a single page. Not a deck, not a model output, but a plain account of the choice, the reasoning, the alternatives, and who owns it, in language a person could read and understand. If you cannot fit it on a page, the odds are you do not yet understand the decision well enough to make it.

The value is that writing it exposes the thinking. A model can hand you an answer that feels complete, and the feeling collapses the moment you try to explain the decision yourself, in your own words, on your own page. The gaps you were gliding over become visible. The parts you were taking on faith show up as parts you cannot actually justify.

This also creates a record that outlasts the moment. Later, you can see what you decided and why, and learn where the tool helped or misled you.

So for decisions that matter, write the page. What is the call, what is the reasoning, what did you consider and reject, who owns it. The act of writing forces the understanding, and understanding is what lets you catch an error. A decision you can explain clearly on one page is one you actually made. A decision you can only point at is one that happened to you.
