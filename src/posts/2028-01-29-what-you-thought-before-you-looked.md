---
title: "What You Thought Before You Looked"
date: 2028-01-29
pageDescription: "If you cannot say it, you did not decide. You deferred."
---
One question tells you whether you actually made a decision or only deferred to one. What did you think before you looked at the tool's answer? If you can say it clearly, you formed a view and weighed the machine against it. If you cannot, you did not decide. You accepted, and called it a decision.

This is the simplest test of whether you are still in the loop. A person deciding has a view of their own to compare against the tool's. A person deferring skips straight to the answer and supplies agreement. From the outside they can look identical, the same click, the same outcome. From the inside they are completely different, and only you can know which one you did.

The value of the question is that it catches the drift toward deferral before it becomes a habit. Asked honestly, it forces you to notice the times you had no independent view at all, when you would have accepted whatever the tool produced.

So ask it, especially on the calls that matter. What did I think before I looked? If the answer is nothing, if you genuinely had no view until the machine gave you one, then the machine decided and you signed. Forming a view first is the small discipline that keeps you the decider, and this question is how you check whether you did.
