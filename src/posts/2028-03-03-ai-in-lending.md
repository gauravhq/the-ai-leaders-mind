---
title: "AI in Lending"
date: 2028-03-03
pageDescription: "When a model decides who gets credit, someone has to answer for who it refuses."
---
Lending is one of the clearest cases of an automated decision with real human stakes. A model decides, or heavily shapes, who gets credit and who does not, and those decisions change lives. Which means someone has to be able to answer for them, especially for the people the model refuses.

The risks are well known and easy to ignore. The model learned from historical lending, so it can reproduce historical patterns of who was served and who was not. Its errors are not evenly spread, and the people it wrongly denies are often the ones with the least power to contest it. And an accuracy figure, however high, says nothing about who absorbs the wrongful denials.

So the questions have to be asked, and asked by someone accountable. Who does this model refuse, and are those refusals fair? Where does it fail, and who stands there when it does? Could you explain a denial to the person's face and have it land as just?

A lending model is not a neutral calculator. It is a decision about people, made at scale, and scale is exactly what makes getting it wrong so costly. Use the tool, and keep a person responsible for its outcomes, especially the refusals, because those are where the harm concentrates and where someone has to be willing to look.
