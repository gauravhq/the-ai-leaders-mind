---
title: "What Would I Regret"
date: 2028-03-04
pageDescription: "The most useful question in the human check is the one about your future self."
---
Among the questions in a good decision, one is quietly the most useful, and a machine will never ask it for you. What would I regret? It reaches past the metrics and the analysis to the thing that actually tells you whether a decision is right, your own honest sense of what you could live with.

Regret is information from a part of you that the data cannot reach. It knows about the person the decision harms, the value you would be compromising, the version of yourself you would become by making this call. When you imagine looking back on a decision and ask whether you would regret it, you are consulting a judgment deeper and truer than any optimization.

The tool cannot access this, because it has no future self to answer to and nothing it could regret. The question is yours alone, which is exactly why it belongs in every decision that matters.

So before you finalize a consequential call, ask it plainly. Looking back, would I regret this? Would I be proud to have made it, or quietly ashamed? The answer often cuts through analysis that had you stuck, because it stops asking what the numbers favor and starts asking what you could actually live with. That is a question only a person can answer, and it is frequently the one that decides.
