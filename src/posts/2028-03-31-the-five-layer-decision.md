---
When you strip away the frameworks, a good decision with AI moves through five layers, whether you name them or not.

First, the machine's input. What does the model say, and how sure is it? Second, your own read, formed independently, so you have something to compare. Third, the gap. Where the two disagree is where the thinking actually happens. Fourth, the human check. Who is affected, what cannot be measured, what would I regret? And fifth, the call, owned by a person who will answer for it.

Skip a layer and you can feel it. Skip your own read and you are just deferring. Skip the gap and you miss the most useful signal in the room. Skip the human check and you optimize your way into a decision you cannot defend.

The layers are not a bureaucracy. Most of the time they take seconds. They are a way of making sure the machine informs the decision without quietly making it. The tool is one voice in the room, an early and powerful voice; the last word stays with the person willing to sign their name to it.
