---
title: "Learning to Learn"
date: 2028-04-08
pageDescription: "The durable skill is not any tool. It is the ability to keep picking up new ones."
---
When the tools change every couple of years, the most valuable thing you can be good at is getting good at things. Learning to learn is the skill under all the specific skills, and it is the one that does not expire when the current tool does.

This sounds abstract until you watch it play out. Two people face a new system. One has deep expertise in the old way and treats the new tool as a threat to be resisted or a burden to be endured. The other is simply good at learning, curious, unattached, willing to be a beginner, and picks it up quickly, then picks up the next one, and the one after that. Over a few cycles, the second person pulls far ahead, not because they were smarter, but because their core skill was adaptation itself.

So invest in the meta-skill. Get good at approaching new things without dread, at being a beginner without shame, at updating what you know rather than defending it. Notice how you learn best and lean into it.

The specific tool you master this year is a depreciating asset. The ability to keep learning is the one that compounds, and in a field that reinvents itself constantly, it is the closest thing to job security there is.
