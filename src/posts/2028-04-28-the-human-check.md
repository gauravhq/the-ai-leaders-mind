---
title: "The Human Check"
date: 2028-04-28
pageDescription: "Before you decide, ask who is affected, what cannot be measured, and what you would regret."
---
There is a layer of a good decision that no model can supply, and it is the human check. After the tool has spoken and you have formed your read and examined the gap, you ask the questions that only a person can ask. Who is affected by this? What matters here that nothing measured? What would I regret?

These questions do not come from the data, because they are about the things the data leaves out. The people behind the numbers, the values at stake, the second-order effects, the sense of what you could live with. A model optimizes the target it was given. The human check is where you step back and ask whether the target, and the answer, actually serve what you care about.

Skipping this layer is how you optimize your way into a decision you cannot defend. The math checks out, the metric improves, and something you should have weighed was never weighed, because it was not the kind of thing the tool could see.

So build the human check in, especially on decisions with real stakes. It takes only a moment and it catches what everything upstream missed. The machine handles what can be measured. The human check is where you account for everything that cannot be, which is often where the decision actually turned.
