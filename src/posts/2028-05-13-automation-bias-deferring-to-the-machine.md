---
title: "Automation Bias: Deferring to the Machine"
date: 2028-05-13
pageDescription: "Deferring to the confident answer feels like trust. Often it is just relief."
---
Automation bias is the quiet habit of trusting the automated answer more than the evidence in front of you. It is why a driver follows the navigation system into a field, and why a clinician can miss what their own eyes would have caught if the screen had not spoken first.

The bias is not stupidity. It is efficiency. Your brain is built to conserve effort, and a confident machine offers a shortcut past the hard work of thinking. Most of the time the shortcut is fine. The danger is the day it is not, and you have stopped looking.

Leaders feel this most when the model agrees with the easy choice. Deferring feels like trust. Often it is just relief.

The counter-move is small and specific. Before you accept a recommendation that matters, state what you would have decided on your own, then compare. If you cannot say what you thought before the model told you, you were not deciding. You were deferring.
