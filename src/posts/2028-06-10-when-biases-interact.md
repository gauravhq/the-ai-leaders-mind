The four biases are dangerous enough on their own. Together they compound.

It usually runs like this. The model names a number, and you anchor to it. The number fits what you hoped, so confirmation bias waves it through. It arrives on the dashboard, so availability bias makes it feel like the only thing that matters. And automation bias means you never quite ask where it came from. Four small nudges, each forgivable, stack into a decision no one actually made on purpose.

This is why fixing one bias at a time rarely holds. You catch yourself anchoring and feel safe, while confirmation quietly does the same work under a different name.

The defense is not a longer checklist. It is a single pause before decisions that matter, where you ask one honest question. What would change my mind here? If nothing could, the machine is not helping you decide. It is helping you rationalize. The pause is short. The habit of taking it is the whole skill.
