---
title: "AI in Strategy Meetings"
date: 2028-07-14
pageDescription: "Let the model inform the room without letting it end the thinking."
---
A capable model in a strategy meeting is a double-edged thing. It can inform the discussion with analysis, options, and considerations no one had assembled. It can also end the thinking, because once its confident answer is on the screen, the room tends to converge on it and the actual strategic debate quietly stops.

The failure is subtle. The tool's output arrives looking neutral and comprehensive, and disagreeing with it starts to feel like arguing with the data. So people defer, the discussion shortens, and a strategy gets adopted because the model suggested it, not because the room thought it through. That is not using the tool. That is letting it decide.

So protect the thinking. Have people form their own views before the model's answer is discussed, so the room brings genuine independent reads rather than a chorus of agreement. Treat the tool's output as one strong input to argue with, not a conclusion to ratify. Ask what it might be missing, and invite the challenge actively.

Strategy is exactly the kind of decision, high-stakes and hard to reverse, that deserves the full human process, not a fast deferral to a machine. Use the model to enrich the room's thinking. Do not let it replace it. The best strategy usually comes from the argument the tool informed, not from the answer it handed over.
