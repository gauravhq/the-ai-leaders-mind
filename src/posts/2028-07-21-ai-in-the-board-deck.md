---
title: "AI in the Board Deck"
date: 2028-07-21
pageDescription: "A tool can build the slides. You still have to understand what they claim."
---
Tools can now assemble a board deck in minutes, pulling data, drafting narrative, generating charts. The efficiency is real and the risk is specific. You can end up presenting, and standing behind, claims you did not actually think through, because the tool produced them and they looked right.

A board deck is not just a document. It is a set of assertions you are vouching for, and the board will ask about them. If the tool built the analysis and you merely presented it, the first hard question exposes that you do not actually understand what you claimed. The polish of the generated deck makes this worse, because it looks authoritative enough to lower your guard about checking it.

So the tool can build the deck, and you still have to own every claim in it. Understand the analysis well enough to defend it without the slides. Know where the numbers came from and what they assume. Be able to answer the question the deck raises, not just read the deck.

Use the machine to save the assembly time, and reinvest that time in understanding, not just in making more slides. The deck is your word to the board. A generated deck you do not understand is a set of claims you cannot defend, dressed up well enough to fool you into presenting them. Understand it first, then let it help you show it.
