---
title: "The Meeting Where No One Disagreed"
date: 2028-09-02
pageDescription: "Smooth agreement around a tool's answer is often the sound of thinking stopping."
---
There is a kind of meeting that feels like success and should worry you. The tool produced an answer, and everyone agreed, and the meeting was short and smooth and conflict-free. That smoothness is often not the sound of a good decision. It is the sound of thinking stopping.

Real decisions usually involve some friction, a dissent, a hard question, a view that had to be worked through. When a machine's answer lands and the room simply accepts it, the friction that would have tested the decision is missing, and its absence is not harmony. It is deference. Everyone quietly agreed with the screen because disagreeing felt like arguing with the data, and the decision got made without actually being examined.

So be suspicious of frictionless agreement around a tool. Ask whether the room genuinely thought it through or just deferred together. Invite the challenge that did not come on its own.

A meeting where no one disagreed with the machine is not necessarily a meeting that reached the right answer. It may be one where no one really decided, where a group outsourced its judgment and mistook the ease of doing so for consensus. The disagreement that did not happen was not a sign of alignment. It was the missing step where the decision would have been tested, and its absence is worth noticing.
