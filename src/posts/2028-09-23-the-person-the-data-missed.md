---
title: "The Person the Data Missed"
date: 2028-09-23
pageDescription: "Behind every clean dataset is someone it failed to see."
---
Every dataset has someone it missed. The person who did not fit the categories, whose situation the fields could not capture, who fell through the gaps in what was collected. The data looks complete, and it is missing them, and a model built on it will make decisions as if they do not exist.

This is easy to forget because absence is invisible. What is in the data is vivid and present. What was left out leaves no trace, so it does not compete for your attention, and you can manage confidently on information that is quietly missing exactly the people who most needed to be seen.

So keep asking who the data missed. The customer too frustrated to answer the survey. The employee whose struggle never made it into a metric. The case that did not fit the form and got dropped. The people who are absent from the data are not absent from the world, and decisions made without them still land on them.

A leader's job is partly to remember the people the data cannot see, and to keep asking about them even when nothing on the screen prompts it. The dataset will never mention who it left out. That is precisely why remembering to ask is a human responsibility, and one that gets more important the more the decisions run on data that is cleaner, and emptier, than the reality it claims to represent.
