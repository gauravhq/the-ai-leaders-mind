---
title: "Measuring the Unmeasurable"
date: 2028-10-27
pageDescription: "The most important capacities cannot be counted. Watch for signals instead."
---
The capacities that matter most as machines take over the measurable work are themselves hard to measure. Judgment, adaptability, the ability to keep learning, the quality of your attention. None of them show up neatly on a dashboard, which tempts people to ignore them in favor of what can be counted. That is a mistake, because the countable things are increasingly the machine's job, and the uncountable ones are yours.

You cannot put a number on judgment, but you can watch for signals of it. Can you still explain your reasoning without the tool? Do your decisions hold up over time? Does your team come to you for the calls that have no clear answer? These are not metrics, but they are evidence, and paying attention to them keeps the important capacities from fading unnoticed.

The danger of measuring only what is easy is that you manage only what is easy, and let the hard, human, uncountable capacities quietly atrophy because nothing was tracking them.

So watch the unmeasurable on purpose, through signals rather than numbers. Notice whether your judgment is sharpening or dulling, whether you are still learning or coasting, whether your attention goes where it should. A dashboard will never show you these, and they are exactly the things that decide whether you stay valuable as the measurable work moves to the machines.
