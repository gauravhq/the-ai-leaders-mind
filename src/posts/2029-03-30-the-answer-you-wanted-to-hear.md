---
title: "The Answer You Wanted to Hear"
date: 2029-03-30
pageDescription: "A tool that can argue anything will happily argue for what you already wanted."
---
Be most careful with the tool exactly when it tells you what you were hoping to hear. A capable model can build a convincing case for almost any position, which means that when you come to it wanting a particular answer, it can hand you a well-argued version of that answer, and the arguing will feel like confirmation when it was really just compliance.

This is the quiet way bias enters. You do not consciously ask the tool to agree with you. You ask a question shaped, a little, by what you want, and the model, being agreeable and fluent, gives you back a persuasive case for it. You walk away more confident, feeling validated by an independent source, when the source just reflected your own wish in better prose.

The tell is the pleasure. When an answer feels purely satisfying, confirming exactly what you hoped with no friction, that is the moment to slow down rather than speed up.

So when the tool tells you what you wanted, treat the wanting as a warning. Ask it to argue the other side. Ask what would make your preferred answer wrong. A model that agrees with your hopes is not confirming them. It is reflecting them, and the more it pleases you, the more carefully you should check whether it found the truth or just found your wish and dressed it up.
