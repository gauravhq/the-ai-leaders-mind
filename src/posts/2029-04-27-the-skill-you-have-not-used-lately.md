---
title: "The Skill You Have Not Used Lately"
date: 2029-04-27
pageDescription: "Somewhere a capability is quietly fading because the tool always does it now."
---
Ask yourself, honestly, what you used to do well that the tool now always does for you. There is probably something, a kind of analysis, a sort of decision, a skill you were once proud of, that you have not actually exercised in months because the machine handles it every time. That skill is not resting. It is fading.

The unsettling part is how invisible the loss is. You feel more productive, the work gets done, and the capability quietly slips because nothing signals its decline. You only discover it is gone the day you need it and reach for it and it is not there, or not what it was.

So the question is worth asking on purpose, because nothing else will ask it for you. What can I no longer do well because I stopped doing it? The answer names the skills quietly at risk, the ones convenience is eroding while you are not looking.

And the answer is also the assignment. The skills you have not used lately are the ones to deliberately use again, now and then, before the fade goes too far to reverse. Do the thing yourself occasionally, even though the tool could. Not out of nostalgia, but because a capability you never exercise is one you are slowly losing, and the only way to keep it is to use it, on purpose, while you still can.
